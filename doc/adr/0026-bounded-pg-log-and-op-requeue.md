# 0026 Bounded PG log (one WAL record per PG txn) and op re-queue on interval change
Status: PG-log bound superseded by 0028 (2026-10-09: multi-record atomic txns, pg log defaults back to Ceph values); op re-queue part stays accepted
Date: 2026-10-09
Context: live all-croit-flashstore-osd lab (M4/M5). (1) A replica receiving a long log slice / whole-log claim persisted it as ONE store txn; with 10000-entry logs the txn exceeded the WAL record (256 KiB), the cold-cache page budget, or the worst-case meta estimate (5 pages/key > a shard's meta region) -> `Rejected`/sticky `NoSpace` -> PG stuck in Activating or OSD exit. (2) After a replica died the primary dropped in-flight ops on the interval change; the client does not resend while the primary is unchanged -> I/O hung forever.
Decision:
- Bound the PG log (and every trim step: `pg_log_trim_max` 50, a trim of a 2000-entry log was one DelRange txn whose estimate overflowed the meta region and killed the primary): `min_pg_log_entries` 100, `max_pg_log_entries` 150, `log_dups_tracked` 150 (300 was still too large for a backfill claim in the lab) (peering `Config`). Every PG txn (log slice, claim, missing delta) stays far below one WAL record in size and page estimate. Splitting a PG txn over several WAL records is REJECTED: a crash between parts would persist a partial PG txn (head/entries/info disagree). Atomic multi-record txns (BEGIN..END units, replay applies only complete chains, ack after END) are the proper way to lift the bound: open work item.
- Consequence: an OSD down for more than ~150 writes per PG is recovered by backfill, not by log. Backfill must therefore be robust (see 90-open-questions).
- Engine: on a new interval at the same primary, unanswered ops (in flight, per-object waiters, parked) are re-queued at the PG gate in arrival order (`Engine::reset`); `on_write_done` with a non-zero result (abort, -EAGAIN) re-queues the op too. Ops that committed are answered by dup detection. Gate cap applies to new ops only; re-queued ops are never dropped.
Gap: lifted by ADR 0028 (BEGIN..END chains in the WAL ring; 0027 was rejected).
Alternatives: chain records in the adapter (partial apply on crash); bigger WAL units (still unbounded); drop and rely on client resend (hangs, observed).
